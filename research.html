Research

Persuasion under Subjective Causal Models

(Job Market Paper)

Can a sender exploit the way a receiver interprets information? I study persuasion when the receiver interprets the sender's signal through a subjective causal model, which the sender knows but does not control. The answer depends on whether she regards the signal as a consequence of the state or as one of its causes. In the first case, the sender never gains from a signal that she misinterprets: he is weakly worse off than against a Bayesian receiver, and she is never worse off than without information. In the second case, her posteriors across signal realizations might not average to the prior. The sender can then be strictly better off, and his gain is the receiver's loss: she can be harmed by information that she values.

Competing in Tailored Stories

[extended abstract]

(joint with C. Aina)

We study the competition among senders in the supply of models. For example, politicians compete to supply voters with models to interpret data on immigration or economic outcomes; brokers might want clients to interpret the same firm’s performance differently, driven by different incentives. The senders' strategies and the receiver’s beliefs vary with respect to the incentives of the parties involved.
